Land Is Harder to Sell Than a House — And Most Owners Find That Out the Hard Way

Vacant land is a different animal from a house. There's no structure, so there's no MLS photo that makes a buyer fall in love. Most banks won't finance raw land the way they'd finance a home, which shrinks the buyer pool dramatically. Real estate agents often aren't eager to list it, because the commission on a small lot doesn't justify the work. So land sits — sometimes for years — while the owner keeps writing checks for property taxes on something they'll never use.

We buy it directly. No listing, no agent, no financing contingency. We pay cash and close on your timeline.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you buy land with no road access? +
Often, yes. Landlocked parcels are hard to sell on the open market, but access issues don't automatically stop us. Tell us about the property and we'll let you know honestly whether we can make an offer.
What if I owe back property taxes on the land? +
That's one of the most common situations we see with land. Delinquent taxes are typically paid off at closing out of the sale proceeds, so you don't need to bring money to the table to clear them first.
I inherited land with my siblings. Can we still sell? +
Yes. We handle heir and succession situations regularly, including properties where multiple family members own a share. In some cases we can even buy a partial interest. Call us and we'll walk through your specific situation.
Do I need a survey before selling? +
Not to get an offer. If a survey ends up being needed to close cleanly, we'll discuss it with you — but you don't need to spend money on one just to find out what your land is worth to us.
I live out of state. Can I still sell my Louisiana land? +
Absolutely — this is very common. Many of the landowners we work with inherited Louisiana property but live elsewhere. Closings can be handled remotely, so you don't need to travel here.
