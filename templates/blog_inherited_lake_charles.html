Inherited Property in Louisiana Goes Through Succession First

Here's the part that surprises most families: in Louisiana, you usually can't sell an inherited house right away, even if everyone agrees to sell. The property is still titled in the name of the person who passed, and Louisiana law requires a legal process called succession (what most other states call probate) to be opened before the heirs have the authority to sell and pass clean title to a buyer. A title company won't insure the sale until that's done.

Succession usually takes anywhere from a couple of months to a year or more, depending on the parish, how complex the estate is, and whether all the heirs are in agreement. That's a long time to keep paying taxes, insurance, and upkeep on a house nobody's living in.

A quick note: We're cash home buyers, not attorneys, and this isn't legal advice. Every estate is different. We can walk you through how a sale typically works and connect the dots, but for the succession itself you'll want a Louisiana succession attorney. The good news: we handle these situations all the time and can line up your cash sale so it's ready to close the moment you have authority.
