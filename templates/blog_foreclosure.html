How Foreclosure Works in Louisiana

Louisiana is one of the few states that still uses a judicial foreclosure process, which means your lender must file a lawsuit and get a court order before selling your home. This actually works in your favor — it takes longer than non-judicial states, giving you more time to find a solution.

Louisiana Foreclosure Timeline

30–90 days missed paymentsLender sends notices of default. You may still be able to reinstate the loan by catching up on payments and fees.
90–120 daysLender files a foreclosure lawsuit (petition) in Louisiana district court. You are formally served.
120–180 daysCourt proceedings. If no defense or resolution, a judgment of foreclosure is entered.
Post-judgmentSheriff's sale (auction) is scheduled — typically 30–60 days after judgment. Once the property sells at auction, it's gone.
At any point before the auctionYou can sell the property, pay off the loan, and stop the foreclosure. This is your window.

Your Options When Facing Foreclosure

Option 1

Loan Reinstatement

Pay all missed payments, late fees, attorney fees, and costs to bring the loan current. This stops foreclosure immediately — but requires coming up with a large lump sum that many homeowners simply don't have.

Option 2

Loan Modification or Forbearance

Negotiate with your lender to modify your loan terms or temporarily pause payments. This takes time and approval is not guaranteed. Works best in early stages before a lawsuit is filed.

Option 3

Do Nothing — Let It Foreclose

The foreclosure goes through, your home sells at auction (usually far below market value), and you walk away with nothing — plus a foreclosure on your credit record for 7 years that affects your ability to rent, borrow, or buy another home.

Option 4 — Most Recommended

Sell Fast for Cash Before the Auction

Sell the property to a cash buyer before the auction date. Your mortgage gets paid off at closing, the foreclosure is stopped, and you walk away with whatever equity remains — plus a voluntary sale (not a foreclosure) on your record, which is far less damaging to your credit.

Why Selling for Cash Beats Foreclosure

Most homeowners in foreclosure don't realize that a voluntary sale — even a fast cash sale below full market value — is dramatically better than letting the bank take the house. Here's why:

  • You may walk away with cash — if you have equity, you receive the difference after the mortgage payoff. In a foreclosure auction, that equity often evaporates
  • Less credit damage — a foreclosure stays on your credit for 7 years and can drop your score by 100–150 points. A short sale or cash sale is treated far less harshly
  • No deficiency judgment risk — in some Louisiana foreclosures, the lender can sue you for the difference if the auction price doesn't cover the loan. A negotiated sale eliminates this
  • You control the timeline — rather than being evicted after an auction, you negotiate a move-out date that works for you
  • It's over fast — close in 7 days and put the entire situation behind you

What If I Owe More Than the Home Is Worth?

This is called being "underwater" on your mortgage, and it's more common than you think — especially on homes in Louisiana that have lost value due to storm damage, deferred maintenance, or market shifts. If you owe more than the home is worth, a traditional sale won't cover your loan balance.

In these cases, we can explore a short sale — where the lender agrees to accept less than the full payoff amount in exchange for a clean settlement. We have experience negotiating short sales with Louisiana lenders and can help navigate this process.

A short sale is not ideal, but it's significantly better than foreclosure for your credit and financial future.

Frequently Asked Questions — Foreclosure in Louisiana

How long does foreclosure take in Louisiana? +
Louisiana's judicial foreclosure process typically takes 6–18 months from first missed payment to sheriff's sale, depending on court backlogs and whether you contest the lawsuit. This gives you meaningful time to sell if you act early.
Can I sell my house after foreclosure proceedings have started? +
Yes — in Louisiana you can sell your home at any point before the sheriff's sale auction, even after a lawsuit has been filed and a judgment entered. The sale pays off the loan and stops the foreclosure.
Will I get any money if I sell during foreclosure? +
It depends on how much equity you have versus what you owe. If the sale price exceeds your mortgage balance plus any liens, you receive the difference at closing. We'll walk you through the numbers honestly before you decide.
What if I have a second mortgage or liens on the property? +
Liens, second mortgages, tax liens, and judgment liens all get paid at closing from the sale proceeds — in order of priority. We work with the title company to make sure this is handled correctly.
How does foreclosure affect my credit compared to a cash sale? +
A completed foreclosure typically drops your credit score 100–150 points and stays on your report for 7 years. A voluntary sale — even a short sale — is recorded differently and has significantly less long-term impact. Many people can qualify for a new mortgage within 2–3 years after a short sale versus 7 years after foreclosure.
